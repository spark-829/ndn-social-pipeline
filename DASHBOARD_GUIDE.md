# How to read the NDN dashboards

For anyone at Nine Degrees North. You don't need to know data or Tableau to use these pages.

Independent analysis of public data. Not made by or for Nine Degrees North.

## Where the numbers come from

Every morning a small program writes down the public numbers you could see yourself on YouTube, TikTok and
Instagram: followers, and views, likes, comments, shares and saves for each post. The dashboards compare today's
numbers with the ones from earlier days. Nothing is guessed or made up.

- We started on 12 Oct 2026 (YouTube), 13 Oct (TikTok) and 15 Oct (Instagram). Until we have a full week, "this week"
  means "since we started", and the page says how many days that is.
- The numbers refresh once a day, in the afternoon (Philippine time).
- Only public numbers. We can't see watch time or who your viewers are. Only NDN's own YouTube Studio and TikTok
  Studio show those.

## Page 0: Executive (the one-minute view)

Read it top to bottom. It answers two questions: **how are we doing this week, and what should we do next?**

**1. Today in one sentence.** The single most important thing in today's numbers, written fresh each afternoon.

**2. Five big numbers.**

| Box | What it means |
|---|---|
| Followers | Everyone following NDN on YouTube, TikTok and Instagram together. The same person can follow on several platforms, so this counts follows, not people. |
| New followers this week | How many followers TikTok and Instagram gained. YouTube is left out, see the note below. |
| Views this week | How many times NDN's YouTube videos and TikTok posts were watched this week. Instagram doesn't show views to the public. |
| New posts this week | Posts published on all three platforms this week. |
| TikTok posts that went big | Out of last month's TikTok posts, the share that got at least twice the views of a normal NDN post from the same time. Higher is better. |

**3. Each platform this week.** One line per platform: followers, new followers, views gained (likes on Instagram),
new posts, and the post that gained the most this week.

**4. What to do next.** Three short actions, written every afternoon from the numbers.

**5. TikTok: how often a post goes big, by month.** Each bar is one month. A taller bar means more of that month's
posts took off. Two good months in a row are worth copying; a falling run of months is an early warning.

**6. TikTok: posts gaining the most views this week.** These can be new posts or old ones that came back. An old
post coming back usually means people are searching for that song or using its sound.

### Why YouTube shows no new subscribers

YouTube doesn't give the exact subscriber count to the public. It shows only the first three digits:
**1.51M** can be anything from 1,510,000 to 1,519,999. So the number only moves when NDN passes the next 10,000
(1.52M). A week with fewer than 10,000 new subscribers looks like zero, even when people did subscribe. NDN
can see the exact number in its own YouTube Studio. To judge YouTube from public numbers, look at **views**:
more than 20 million in the week from 12 to 19 Oct.

## Words used on the pages

| Word | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Normal post | The middle result of the 30 NDN posts published closest in time. Half did better, half did worse. |
| Went big / breakout | A post with at least twice the views of a normal post from its time. |
| Performance index (TikTok page) | A post's views divided by a normal post's views. 1 = normal, 2 = twice as many, 0.5 = half. It's fair to old and new posts. |
| Views per day | Total views divided by days since posting. Fair between old and new videos. |
| Shares + saves per 1,000 views | Out of every 1,000 people who watched, how many sent it to someone or saved it. TikTok treats these as strong signs a post is good. |
