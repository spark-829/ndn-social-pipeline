NDN Pipeline: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 3 November 2026

NDN Pipeline is a private analysis tool built by Vicson Yaris. It helps understand how videos on the Nine Degrees North (NDN) YouTube channel perform. It is not an official NDN product, and it is not a public service: only the channel owner can connect it.

What the app reads

If the channel owner clicks Allow, the app can read YouTube Analytics reports for the NDN channel, such as views, watch time, audience retention and where viewers come from. It asks for one permission only: https://www.googleapis.com/auth/yt-analytics.readonly. This is read-only. The app cannot change, upload, delete or comment on anything, and it cannot see the owner's password.

What the app does not collect

The app does not collect personal information about individual viewers. YouTube Analytics reports are totals and groups (for example, views per day or viewers per country), not names or accounts.

Where the data is stored

The reports are stored in a private database hosted on Supabase, under an account controlled by Vicson Yaris. The sign-in token that lets the app fetch reports is stored in the same database and is not visible to the public.

How the data is used

The data is used only to produce internal charts and reports about NDN's channel. It is never sold, rented, shared with advertisers or used for advertising. It is not shared with other people except the NDN team.

The app's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

How to stop access

The channel owner can remove the app at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions: choose NDN Pipeline and then Delete all connections. The app stops being able to read the channel straight away. The owner can also revoke access in YouTube Studio under Settings, Permissions.

Deleting data

To have the YouTube Analytics reports and the sign-in token that this app stored deleted, email the contact address below. They will be deleted within 30 days. This does not cover public information that anyone can see on YouTube, such as video titles and public view counts.
